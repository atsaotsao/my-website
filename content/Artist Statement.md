I work primarily with ink and abstraction to give form to what is usually felt but unseen: the mystical, the ancestral, the inner landscape. My body of work is centered on restoring relationship—with self, with lineage, with land—and remembering our deepest desires as living seeds for building new worlds together.

My work also responds to experiences of displacement and the lingering colonial wounds carried through my ancestral lineage, especially the rage and grief of being unrooted from our homelands and forced to adapt, disassociate, hide, assimilate, and survive.

My practice is deeply informed by lineage and self-directed study. My work draws from Buddhist and Zen philosophies, East Asian ink practices, earth-based wisdom, as well as somatic and mindfulness-based approaches to creating and being. It is also informed by the coaching and group facilitation work I do, alongside my own ongoing experiments in embracing surrender in daily life.

While ink serves as a foundation, my practice often expands into mixed media. Cutting, layering, and introducing color have emerged as ways of responding when the work begins to disintegrate, fragment, or ask for return. These material shifts mirror the tension between containment and release that runs throughout my work.

The work invites viewers to slow down—perhaps noticing the imperfections of ink brush trails, or observing what arises in the body when witnessing unexplainable shapes and forms. I’m interested in whether the work can bring someone back to that inner ember, however briefly.
