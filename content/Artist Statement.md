Andrew Tsao (曹明鑫) is a Taiwanese American artist and coach who works with ink, abstraction, and people to give form to what is often felt but unseen—the mystical, the ancestral, the inner landscape. His work tends to the relationships between self, ancestry, and land, and on remembering desire and imagination as living seeds for building new worlds together.

He approaches each work through playful improvisation, brush moving before the mind can intervene, letting instinctive gestural movements guide the hand. Circles and curves appear throughout, early shapes that became a kind of vocabulary: a way of breaking open and of beginning again.

Tsao's practice moves fluidly between writing, movement, music, and visual art, with each informing the other and each leaving traces. His layered mixed media work reflects this woven quality, building through color and texture the way a piece of music builds through repetition and variation. His visual language draws from a wide constellation of influences: the spiritual abstraction of Hilma af Klint and Wassily Kandinsky, the ink-rooted contemporary vision of Bingyi and Matthew Wong, the tender structural poetry of Ruth Asawa.

Rooted in the dance between Eastern and Western ways of knowing and being—between collective and individual, discipline and improvisation, striving and stillness—his work invites viewers, especially those of the Asian diaspora, to shed the inherited shapes that contained them and remember that creativity, peace, and ancestral gifts were never out of reach.
