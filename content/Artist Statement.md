I work primarily with ink and abstraction to give form to what is usually felt but unseen: the mystical, the ancestral, the inner landscape. My body of work is centered on restoring relationship—with self, with lineage, with land—and remembering our deepest desires and dreams as living seeds for building new worlds together.

My work also responds to experiences of displacement and to the lingering colonial wounds carried through my ancestral lineage, as well as the daily tension of surviving and creating within late capitalism.

My practice is deeply informed by lineage and self-directed study. While I am self-taught, my work draws from Buddhist and Zen philosophies, East Asian ink practices, alongside somatic and mindfulness-based approaches to creating and being. It is also constantly informed by the coaching and group facilitation work I do, as well as my own ongoing experiments in embracing surrender in my day-to-day life.

While ink serves as a foundation, my practice often expands into mixed media. Cutting, layering, and introducing color has been emergent ways of responding to moments of fragmentation, upheavals, or repair. These material shifts mirror the tension between containment and release that runs throughout my work.

The work invites viewers to slow down—perhaps noticing the imperfections of ink brush trails, or observing what arises in the body when witnessing unexplainable shapes and forms. Ultimately, I hope the viewer experiences a deeper connection to their inner ember, where love, light, and intuition feel most present.
