My friends all know that I like to talk about music. Hell, I like talking about it so much that I even decided to publish an entire monthly newsletter to talk about it more. But in the many years of sharing and discussing a wide range of songs, albums, and genres, my friends have definitely never heard me talk about hip-hop. 

At the risk of sounding like an uncultured person, I didn’t feel like I “understood” hip-hop for the longest time. One of my favorite podcasts is Jon Caramonica’s [NYT podcast on music](https://www.nytimes.com/column/popcast-pop-music-podcast) and every year him and his music journalist friends will discuss their favorite albums of the year and they would often include a handful of hip-hop albums as one of their top five. I would always remember thinking to myself, “What am I missing? And why do I have so much difficulty _truly_ appreciating this genre?”

This all changed last May. I carved out an intentional day for myself on a Saturday to shut down my digital devices and spent the entire day doing creative things that nurtured me which included laying down in my living room and listening to albums. One of the albums I chose was Kendrick Lamar’s [_To Pimp a Butterfly_](https://open.spotify.com/album/7ycBtnsMtyVbbwTfJwRjSP?si=uVBf4ccFSGur7V6SKPbO9g). I am not exaggerating when I say this, that even though I’ve heard specific songs from the album before, that particular experience of listening to it from beginning to end completely cracked open another dimension of my appreciation, curiosity, and adoration of the genre. Listening to Kendrick’s masterful album in its entirety shifted something in me, and I found myself experiencing a magnus opus so powerful in scope, message, and artistic beauty that it left me absolutely speechless. In the following weeks, I spent hours upon hours listening to a [podcast](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/dissect/episodes/S1E1--Compton--K-Dot--and-Kendrick-Lamar-e1nsbo) dissecting each song and the meaning behind it. I was obsessed. Some part of me felt ashamed that I had been missing out all this time, but mostly, I just felt exhilarated that a new world of music had opened up to me. 

This month’s curation is the first in which I’ve focused primarily on hip-hop. Along with some deeply soulful R&B tracks, I’ve hand selected a range of rap and hip-hop songs to feature some recent favorites that got me bopping while also showcasing some of its history. The playlist is a celebration of the good things in life that open up to us when we have the intention and courage to explore outside of our comfort zone.
