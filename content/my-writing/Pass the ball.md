Next time when you are at a social gathering, preferably at like a dinner table where everyone can see and interact with each other, try to sit back quietly and pretend that the "flow" of conversation is a ball of bright light. Start picturing it in your head, then try to actually "see" it going back and forth between people. When someone is telling a story, they're holding that ball. When someone is asking someone another question, they're passing that ball to him or her. In my years of occasionally performing this observational experiment, I notice one very important and very intriguing thing:

**Everyone wants the ball, but people rarely pass each other the ball.**

Here's how it often goes: the extroverts often start the game off with hogging the ball, telling stories and sharing anecdotes; charming the crowd and keeping the energy up. They are the ones that often keep that ball unless someone takes it from them. The introverts will learn to remember important parts of the things that the extroverts say and latch on to one particular point to use it to insert themselves into the "flow" of the conversation. They will either talk about something similar ("I have a story that's just like that..") or use it to pivot to an area of topic that they have a lot to talk about ("Speaking of that thing..."). At the end of the day, its a game of how do I steal the ball, and if you don't ever feel courageous enough to step in, you get left out.

Now imagine a different scenario, imagine the game of group conversation as a team sport exactly like basketball, where it's more important to ask questions than to go into a monologue; to pass rather than to keep the ball. How much better for the quality of conversation be? Wouldn't that create an environment where no one is left out? Wouldn't that teach us to listen more than to speak? And ultimately at the end of the night, wouldn't we actually get to know each other on a deeper level?Try doing that more next time you're at a social event. Be that person who passes the ball.
