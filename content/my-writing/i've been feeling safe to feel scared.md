I've been feeling safe to feel scared. 

It sounds paradoxical but feels real in my body. For the bulk of the self work I've been doing these years, I've been slowly, steadily, and patiently asking my protector selves and parts to loosen the grip of the steering wheel, and instead learn to step aside to sit in the backseat, or at least in the shotgun. 

Most days I wonder, "Who am I without these protective and survival instincts that helped me get here? That helped my parents, my grandparents, and even ancestors find safety?" 

What if I can change the ways I relate to safety?

This practice humbles me. I've learned that these protectors have a whole team. So many shapes of these body guards will leap in to protect and prevent me from fully facing and feeling the fear and anxiety when faced with the extent of suffering all around us.

Anger was the general. Sadness and victimhood were the lieutenants. Optimism and depression were undercover agents. All had good intentions, but when they finally stepped aside, and when I swam even closer to the soft inner core, I'm surprised at what I found. I'm surprised to find an anxious boy.

I wouldn't consider myself a very anxious person, so finding this has been disorienting. But it felt freeing to continue be so honest with myself and my body.  It feels freeing to finally say to this boy, "I'm sorry you never felt safe to feel scared. But you're safe now and I'm here."

I'm here. 
And thank you for being here with me.
