I'm an ideas guy. In fact, I believe we are all are idea individuals who innately have the capacity and creativity to generate a new output, suggestion, solution, or idea based on the scenario presented to us. This exists for both kids and adults, and the problem lies with whether he or she is in an environment that supports and gives permission for that idea to be expressed and even acted upon. The moment where the idea is rejected, worse, even suppressed over and over again, the individual loses the ability to believe in the validity and creativity in one's ideas and stop thinking or acting on them.

I experienced this first-hand in my very first job. I landed a job as a door-to-door salesman because the business owner was impressed with my sales ability, and found myself working at a small business that sold high-end custom-made jewelry safes. For the most part, it was mostly just the business owner and me, and even though the business was relatively straightforward, I remember I always had ideas to find ways to transform and innovate from within. These include introducing tech to help with processes. This includes some productivity suggestions here and there or even suggestions to build a new website. I quickly realized working for a micro-manger who likes control meant that a lot of these things were shut down without much justification. When this happens over and over again, loops after loops, I remember being hyper-aware that my ideas at the start seem to get stuck at the bottom of my through, later in my chest, and sooner and later I stopped having them like a dreamless sleep. I stopped having those ideas because I didn't care anymore and it was easier to survive in that environment without them.

As I take a look at a lot of my friends, colleagues, and fellow professionals around me, I noticed that this is happening to a lot of them. More than once I have heard individuals talk about not ever seeing themselves start anything, even a side-project, because they don't think they have a good idea to act on. What they don't realize is that that joy and ability have been eroded out of them. 

This is one of the things I am trying to tackle in my coaching. My goal is to use the accessibility of no-code tools plus any existing digital tools (like even google sheets) to remind people of the power of having, building, and then sharing one's idea. It's more than just about getting users, or passive income, building wealth, or solving problems, at the end of the day it's also about personal healing.
