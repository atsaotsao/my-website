This question came up over to-go burger and fries between a friend and me. It started with a fervent discussion over the [very recent logo changes](https://9to5google.com/2020/02/06/google-maps-redesign-app-logo-birthday/) of the Google Maps app icon. The core question about the change was, was it necessary or was it trivial? Then it transitioned to a more philosophical question about whether there are products today, especially digital products like websites, platforms, and apps that are good exactly the way they are today and do not need any significant upgrades in terms of new features or new design?

This question absolutely stumped me. As a product-oriented person, it is in my DNA to think about products in terms of iterations, where it technically means a product is never complete; there can always be ways to improve upon it. That makes sense, but is it necessary? Was it necessary for [Reddit to change their beloved OG design](https://www.wired.com/story/reddit-redesign/)? Do we need Google maps to continue adding features now around user-generated content? What about Cowriters.app? Do we need any new features to allow us to better keep our habit of writing daily?

We iterate and continue building on what people want because of the often stark message of "innovate or die." If you stop, it will one day become stagnant, and ultimately what that means is that users will flock to something better; make sense. But I can't help but marvel at the beauty of "products" that once been released, no longer need any edits, modifications, and additions. These are the masterpieces of albums, video games, and books that are rewatched, replayed and reread over and over again despite all the flaws from it that come with time, context, and technology.

Perhaps it's important to think and build technology in that context; ones that can be completed and you move on to the next. Who knows, if it's important enough, you'll get a chance to work on it again in 20 years for an remastered version.
